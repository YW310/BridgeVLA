OHT wheel assembly data processing and training

Data processing

The real-robot wheel assembly archive contains 248 successful recordings from manual collection, augmentation and DAgger. We converted and validated 20 recordings and 5,676 samples as a V4 replay pilot, with 2,897 training, 1,358 validation and 1,421 test samples.

  1. Matched RGB and native depth by capture index for the two fixed cameras. Registered RGB to the depth grid where needed, converted depth to meters and transformed both point clouds into the UR robot base frame. Each robot observation uses camera frames captured at or before its timestamp and at most 150 ms earlier.
  2. Paired images with measured joint angles, tool center point (TCP) poses and gripper opening. Built action labels from the original robot timestamps, with up to 16 future steps at nominal 20 ms intervals. Each labeled keypoint targets the next measured gripper transition. Sampling retains valid frames at gripper transitions.
  3. Kept related recordings and exact duplicates in the same split. Checked that all 83 explicit replay and DAgger source links stayed within a split, and traced 2,343 pilot action labels back to the raw robot measurements.

Training and model error

The validated replay ran through V4 fine-tuning, checkpoint reload and offline inference. We reused the simulation encoder and trained the keypoint output layers and a newly initialized action-chunk decoder with sine and cosine joint features. The pilot completed eight optimizer updates on a 16 GB RTX 5080 with 9.45 GiB of peak PyTorch reserved memory.

We evaluated the saved checkpoint on 120 validation and 144 test observations, sampling 24 per recording. The first four steps cover a nominal 80 ms and all 16 cover 320 ms. The baseline holds the current TCP position.

The four training observations used in the final optimizer update averaged 1.78 mm over the first four steps and 5.93 mm over all 16.

On the test set, the keypoint head averaged 0.76 m of position error, with 61 of 104 labeled predictions outside the workspace. The first four steps contained zero gripper transitions in the evaluation samples.

Next steps

Five of six pilot test recordings share an assistance reference with training recordings. Group recordings by these references and complete the full replay before a longer fine-tune. Recheck prediction error and gripper transitions before physical-robot closed-loop evaluation.
