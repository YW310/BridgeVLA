Background & Motivation

The previous OHT study focused on discrete keypoint prediction, establishing an open-loop baseline with a mean translation error of 7.79 mm and a mean rotation error of 1.39°. While keypoints specify the next manipulation target, they do not explicitly describe the continuous motion required to reach it.

Building on this baseline, our recent work extends OHT from discrete keypoint prediction to continuous action-chunk prediction, together with the corresponding data, execution, and evaluation pipeline.

Summary

  • Established the OHT execution and action-chunk data pipeline: Implemented the IsaacLab Client/Server interface, expert replay, execution control, and data support for joint keypoint and action-chunk prediction.
  • Implemented state- and keypoint-conditioned trajectory prediction: Evaluated a delta-pose regression model with Keypoint Guidance and TCP-centered local context; explored Flow Matching as an alternative trajectory generator.
  • Completed full-episode open-loop prediction evaluation: Evaluated 405 cached observations across Episodes 17 and 31, obtaining mean position errors of 0.91 mm and 1.58 mm, respectively, over the first four future steps.

Results

1. OHT Action-Chunk Dataset and Execution Pipeline

  • Execution support: Implemented the IsaacLab Client/Server interface, expert-trajectory replay, waypoint reach checks, and gripper stabilization control.
  • Action-chunk dataset: Built training data, training configurations, and a timed execution interface for joint prediction of keypoints and continuous action segments.
  • Object annotations: Generated task-relevant object annotations and replay data using the provided segmentation ground truth, preparing for future object-conditioned OHT experiments.

Method

1. State- and Keypoint-Conditioned Action-Chunk Prediction

  • State conditioning: The evaluated model uses deterministic delta-pose regression conditioned on normalized current joint and gripper states. Predicted incremental poses are composed into world-frame TCP targets.
  • Keypoint Guidance: The predicted key goal conditions action-chunk generation, linking short-term motion to the next manipulation target.
  • TCP-centered local context: Local visual context around the measured TCP supports short-horizon trajectory prediction.

Flow Matching is explored separately as an alternative trajectory generator. The reported evaluation uses the regression model; the contribution of individual conditioning mechanisms remains to be established through matched ablations.

2. Prediction and Execution Diagnosis

Generate independent action-chunk predictions from recorded observations and compare them with future ground-truth trajectories and a stationary baseline. Analyze per-step and horizon-wise position errors without executing the predictions. Separately use GT replay to isolate controller tracking, and recursive predicted-TCP feedback with other recorded inputs held fixed to inspect feedback sensitivity.
